I joined the [Computational Privacy Group](https://cpg.doc.ic.ac.uk/#hero-area) at Imperial College London in June 2025 as a Research Fellow. Working with [Yves-Alexandre de Montjoye](https://demontjoye.com) and [Matthew Wicker](https://www.mattrwicker.com). My research focuses on the robustness of machine learning systems, developing evaluation methods and formal frameworks that predict how models and multi-agent AI pipelines fail when deployed in adversarial or real-world conditions. I study this across two settings: how ML models used for identification and classification tasks degrade when the environment shifts, and how multi-agent AI systems can be compromised at the pipeline level even when each component appears robust. My current work covers privacy attacks against ML models and data releases, LLM fingerprinting, and adversarial robustness of multi-agent AI pipelines.

I was awarded my PhD in May 2025 at [Adaptive Emergent Systems Engineering Laboratory (AESE)](https://wp.doc.ic.ac.uk/aese/) in the [Department of Computing](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/computing/) at Imperial College London, under the supervision of [Professor Julie McCann](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/people/j.mccann). My doctoral research evaluated the robustness of ML-based IoT device identification systems under real deployment conditions, including device mode changes, spatial and temporal distribution shift, and sampled network traffic.

In 2017, I completed my MSc in Electromagnetic Sensor Networks at the University of Birmingham. The focus of the Master was on electromagnetic, antennas, propagation, computer communications networks, and RF and microwave engineering. Moreover, I completed my BA in Computer Systems Engineering from Birzeit University in Palestine.
